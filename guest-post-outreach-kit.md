## ⚠️ Read this first — what a GOOD guest post is

A guest post is **one genuinely useful article on a real, relevant blog** that links back to your site once or twice. That's it.

**This is a marathon, not a sprint.**
- Target: **1–2 quality posts per month.** Not 10. Quality beats quantity enormously.
- A single link from a respected dev blog (e.g., Smashing Magazine, freeCodeCamp, CSS-Tricks) is worth more than 100 links from random sites.
- Every article must be something you'd be proud to publish under your own name. Real value, real expertise, no fluff.

**🚫 NEVER do these (they get you penalized or banned):**
- ❌ **Never buy guest posts or links.** Google penalizes paid links. "Write an article for $50 and we'll link you" = paid link = risk.
- ❌ **Never use an article spinner or AI-generated filler.** Editors can spot it instantly; it destroys your reputation.
- ❌ **Never submit to a "write for us" link farm.** If a site publishes anything from anyone with no editorial standards, its links are worthless (or harmful).
- ❌ **Never use the same pitch email for 100 sites.** Personalize every single one.
- ❌ **Never stuff your article with links.** 1–2 contextual links maximum. Usually just 1 in the author bio.

## PHASE 2 — Pick a topic you can own

The article must be something your ideal client (a business founder/decision-maker) would genuinely find useful — and that showcases your expertise. Don't write generic "10 tips" listicles; write from real experience.

**Proven topic ideas (pick what you've actually done):**
1. "How we built a [SaaS MVP / custom Laravel app] for a client in [country] — and what it cost"
2. "Website vs. web app vs. SaaS: which does your business actually need?"
3. "The real cost of building a web app in 2026 (with real project numbers)"
4. "5 signs your business has outgrown its website (and needs a web app)"
5. "How to choose a web development partner abroad without getting burned"
6. "Laravel vs. Node vs. WordPress: choosing a stack for your business product"
7. "What we learned shipping our first SaaS MVP in [X] weeks"
8. "A founder's guide to scoping a software project before you hire anyone"

**Why these work:** They're specific, experience-based, and directly relevant to the clients you want. Generic content gets ignored; specific, honest content gets shared and linked to.

## PHASE 4 — What to do AFTER they say yes

This is where most people waste the opportunity. Get the link right.

**✅ The article rules:**
- Deliver **early** and exactly as promised. Reputation with editors compounds.
- Make it the best thing on their site. Over-deliver.
- **1 link to your site, naturally placed** — e.g., "at my studio, Sky Web Innovations, we've found…" with the link on "Sky Web Innovations" or a genuinely relevant resource like your cost calculator.
- **1 link in the author bio.** Both are normal and accepted.
- ❌ **Do NOT** put 5 links in, or use exact-match money keywords like "hire web developers India" as anchor text. That looks manipulative. Use your brand name or a natural phrase.

**✅ The author bio (use this):**
> **Aakash Thakkar** is the founder of [Sky Web Innovations](https://skywebinnovations.com), a web development studio building websites, web applications, and SaaS products for businesses across the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. He writes about building software products and the real cost of development.

## PHASE 5 — Track and measure

Update your tracker after every touch. When a post goes live:

1. **Get the live URL** and add it to your tracker.
2. **Share it** on LinkedIn (tag the blog) — this drives traffic *to* their site, which editors love, and gets you invited back.
3. **Watch Google Search Console** over the next 2–8 weeks — referral traffic + the backlink will register.
4. **Ask about the next one** once the post performs: "That piece did well — got another idea for next month?"
